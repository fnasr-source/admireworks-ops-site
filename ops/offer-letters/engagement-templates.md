# Engagement clause variants

VERBATIM from the real 2026 letters, extracted 2026-08-08. REPLACES the
2026-08-07 reconstruction, which invented a "This is a full-time position based
{location}. Your working days are {...}. You will report to the {...}." frame
that appears in no real letter and duplicated the reports-to line that actually
lives in the OPENING paragraph.

The clause sits immediately after the opening paragraph. Older letters (2024)
omit it entirely.

These are NOT two fills of one frame. They are structurally different clauses:
the full-time variant carries location + working days + hours; the part-time
variant carries duration + conversion + supersession and NO location or hours.

## full-time (canonical, Sally Tarek 2026-05-16)

```
This engagement is full-time and on-site at our {office_location} office, {working_days} during standard business hours.
```

As sent:
> This engagement is full-time and on-site at our Cairo office, Sunday through Thursday during standard business hours.

`Sunday through Thursday` is the EGYPT working week. For a Dubai hire the same
week applies; for a remote or European hire it does not. Do not default it
blindly.

## part-time converting to full-time (Nour Yassen 2026-05-10)

```
This engagement is part-time for the first {initial_period}, transitioning to a full-time role thereafter under a separate agreement that will supersede and replace this one.
```

As sent:
> This engagement is part-time for the first three months, transitioning to a full-time role thereafter under a separate agreement that will supersede and replace this one.

NEGOTIATED, not a default. The supersession promise is a real commitment: only
use it when that conversion has actually been agreed.

## contractor / freelance

No verbatim example exists in the corpus read (the Youssef Khafaga "Freelance
Performance Marketer" offer of 2025-11-19 was not among the letters extracted).
Derive from the full-time clause when first needed, get owner approval, then
record the approved wording here rather than improvising again.

## omitted

Legitimate: the 2024 generation had no engagement clause at all. If the terms
are captured elsewhere, omitting is a valid choice, not an error.
